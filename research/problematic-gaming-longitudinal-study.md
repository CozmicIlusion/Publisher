# Research Notes — Problematic Gaming and Early Adolescent Risk Factors

**Intended article vertical:** Gaming  
**Primary source:** Mürner-Lavanchy et al., *Journal of Behavioral Addictions* (2025), DOI: 10.1556/2006.2025.00068  
**Public abstract:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40875492/

## Verifiable Findings

The six-year prospective study assessed temperament in 245 participants and brain volume in 154 participants at ages 11–13, then measured a dimensional problematic-video-gaming score in 130 participants at ages 17–19. Negative affectivity was associated with later problematic video gaming. In male participants, lower effortful control was also associated with higher later problematic-gaming scores. Exploratory models reported modest evidence involving effortful control and amygdala volume.

## Essential Editorial Limits

The study is observational and does **not** establish that gaming causes negative affectivity, that brain volume determines gaming behavior, or that these results apply equally to every player. The later problematic-gaming analysis used 130 participants; the authors explicitly state that generalizability and interaction findings require cautious interpretation and replication.

## Article Angle

> The internet keeps asking whether gaming makes young people unhealthy. A six-year study suggests the more useful prevention question is which young people arrive at gaming already vulnerable — and whether games are becoming the place they go to regulate feelings that other support systems have not handled.

## Supporting Context Source

Sun et al., *Frontiers in Neuroscience* (2025), reviewed 17 studies of sleep deprivation and cognitive flexibility. It is not a gaming study and will not be used to make claims about gaming. It may be cited only in a clearly bounded context about the importance of sleep and self-regulation if that context is necessary; this article will avoid such a tangential claim.
